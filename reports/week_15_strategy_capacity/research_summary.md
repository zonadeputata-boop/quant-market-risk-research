# Research summary

Frazzini, Israel and Moskowitz use nearly $1 trillion of live institutional
trades across 19 developed equity markets and show that costs and break-even
fund sizes differ substantially by strategy. Value and momentum scale better
than short-term reversal in their setting, and cost-aware portfolio design can
increase capacity.

Toth, Eisler and Bouchaud report empirical support for a square-root impact law
in options and summarize related evidence across stocks and futures. The
specification relates impact to volatility and the square root of executed
volume relative to daily volume.

Zarinelli, Treccani, Farmer and Lillo find that the square-root specification is
only approximate across a broad range of U.S. equity metaorders. Their impact
surface gives separate roles to participation and execution duration. This is
why the Week 15 model presents sensitivity analysis instead of a precise
capacity forecast.

AQR's 2025 practitioner paper distinguishes explicit and implicit costs and
explains why the benchmark matters. A pre-trade benchmark can capture spread
and market impact, but reliable estimates often require many executions.
Opportunity costs from unfilled orders should also enter the review.

Ivashchenko and Kosowski provide a recent corporate-bond application. They
define capacity as the fund size where average net return reaches zero and show
that turnover and implementation constraints materially change capacity.
