# Research summary

## Result

The post's central claim is supported: option-implied volatility has historically tended to exceed subsequent realized volatility. Cboe reports an average 4.3-volatility-point difference from 1990 through 2014.

## Interpretation

The difference is compensation for bearing volatility, jump and tail risk, not an arbitrage profit. A sell-volatility strategy can earn premium in ordinary periods while remaining exposed to rare, nonlinear losses.

## Important correction

VIX minus subsequent realized volatility is a descriptive realized gap. The theoretical variance risk premium is defined using expected variance under risk-neutral and physical probability measures. Because the physical expectation is unobservable, its estimate is model-dependent.

## Forecasting evidence

S&P DJI/Cboe's 1990-2017 comparison shows raw VIX had a 5.27-point average absolute forecast error. Its VCR calibration reduced the reported error to 3.58 points, a 32.1% reduction in this source-reported comparison. The number is descriptive and is not presented as a new out-of-sample result.

## Currentness review

Cboe's current VIX product page continues to describe the long-run implied-over-realized tendency. A 2024 Review of Financial Studies replication finds that variance-premium return predictability remains, while warning that some early estimates were exaggerated by measurement and specification choices. The post therefore avoids claiming a stable or guaranteed trading edge.
