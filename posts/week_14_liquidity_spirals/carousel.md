# Week 14 carousel

## 1. Liquidity Spirals

When margin calls force the next sale.

## 2. Two forms of liquidity

Market liquidity describes how much a trade moves the price. Funding liquidity describes the cash and collateral available to keep a position open. A liquidity spiral begins when stress in one weakens the other.

## 3. The feedback loop

Price loss. Higher leverage. Margin or collateral call. Forced sale. Price impact. The balance sheet returns to the same constraint and the loop can continue.

## 4. Shock and leverage

The example starts with $250 of assets, $150 of debt and $100 of equity. A 4% asset shock leaves $240 of assets and $90 of equity. Leverage rises from 2.50x to 2.67x. Restoring the limit requires a $15 sale.

## 5. One shock, three feedback strengths

The no-feedback scenario requires $15.0 of cumulative sales. The lower-feedback scenario requires $21.0. The stronger-feedback scenario requires $35.3. The impact sensitivities are assumptions.

## 6. Price impact and repeated sales

Under stronger feedback, the first $15 sale restores 2.50x leverage at the current price. The modeled price impact then lowers equity to $84.4 and raises leverage to 2.60x. Further sales continue until the constraint is restored.

## 7. The 2022 gilt-market episode

The Bank of England estimated more than £70 billion of margin and collateral calls. From 23 September to 14 October, LDI funds sold around £23 billion of gilts and DB pension schemes around £14 billion.

## 8. Liquidity-spiral stress test

Apply the market shock. Revalue positions, collateral and margin. Calculate the cash gap. Map required sales to executable market depth. Apply price impact and repeat.

## 9. Scope of the result

The scenario illustrates a feedback mechanism. It does not estimate market-impact coefficients or event probabilities. A risk review also needs cash buffers, collateral eligibility, liquidation capacity, funding access and the likely actions of other constrained investors.
