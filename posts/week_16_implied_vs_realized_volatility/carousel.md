# Week 16 carousel: Implied vs. Realized Volatility

## 1. Implied vs. Realized Volatility

Why the gap is not free alpha.

## 2. Two measures, two time directions

Implied volatility is inferred from option prices today. Realized volatility is measured from returns after the period has passed. A useful comparison aligns the same underlying, horizon and annualization.

## 3. A fair comparison

At time t, observe the VIX. From t through the next 30 calendar days, measure S&P 500 realized volatility. The simple difference is a descriptive ex-post gap. The theoretical variance risk premium compares risk-neutral and physical expected variance.

## 4. The historical average gap was positive

Cboe reported 1990-2014 averages of 19.9 for VIX and 15.6 for subsequent realized volatility: a gap of 4.3 volatility points.

## 5. Raw VIX is not a direct forecast

For 1990-2017, S&P DJI/Cboe reported an average absolute error of 5.27 volatility points for raw VIX versus 3.58 for its calibrated VCR approach. Calibration helped, but did not create a crystal ball.

## 6. What the premium compensates

Insurance demand, jump and crash risk, volatility-of-volatility, correlation spikes, dynamic-hedging costs and balance-sheet constraints.

## 7. When realized volatility wins

Implied volatility is known up front. Realized volatility is known only afterward and can be higher. A short-volatility strategy can face gap losses, negative skew, margin pressure and forced deleveraging.

## 8. Evidence and limits

The average gap is well documented, but the exact premium is model-dependent. Return-predictability evidence varies with sample and specification. Costs, strike, maturity, skew and implementation determine tradable P&L.

## 9. Volatility review checklist

Align the underlying and horizon. Use future realized volatility. Compare variance consistently. Separate forecast error from risk premium. Stress jumps, volatility-of-volatility, liquidity and margin.
